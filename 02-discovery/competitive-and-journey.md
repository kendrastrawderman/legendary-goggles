# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A long-time streaming subscriber who wants to discover great new content but feels overwhelmed by the size of the catalog.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Find something worth watching quickly without spending excessive time browsing.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** The user experiences decision paralysis because the platform offers thousands of titles but little meaningful guidance, leading to long browsing sessions that end without watching anything. This is reflected in users who reported scrolling for 20 minutes before giving up, repeatedly rewatching the same comfort shows because discovery feels like work, and describing the catalog as a "warehouse" instead of a curated experience.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Recommendations from friends (UXR-10)
Curated recommendations from a competing streaming service's email newsletter (UXR-04)
Falling back to familiar content by rewatching favorite shows instead of discovering something new (UXR-03)
Abandoning streaming entirely in favor of previously owned media (e.g., DVDs) (UXR-01)
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Step 1: Open the streaming app intending to find something to watch.

Step 2: Browse multiple recommendation rows and search results for an extended period (often 20+ minutes), attempting to identify something appealing.

Step 3: Lose confidence in the platform's recommendations because they feel repetitive, overwhelming, or poorly personalized.

Step 4: Leave the platform and rely on external sources to decide what to watch.

Step 5: Return only after already deciding what to watch—or never return, choosing a competing service instead.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** outsourcing content discovery
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** search results for an extended period, lose confidence in the platform's recommendations
